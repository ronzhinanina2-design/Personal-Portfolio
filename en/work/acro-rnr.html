Competition registration and payment system for 15,000 athletes

Competitions ran on a very outdated interface that was inconvenient for everyone. I designed a new system for registering athletes and handling applications and payments. It launched for Russian competitions, and international ones joined later.

Role

I adapted and updated the Prime One design library and made most of the layouts. The design lead and I made the customer journey map together. The lead reviewed my layouts, I made the changes, and we presented them to the PM on the client's side together. The interface was designed in English first. I translated it into Russian and wrote the translation rules for the developers. The team also had six developers and two testers.

The task

The old system was very outdated and inconvenient for everyone. It was rebuilt from scratch, with many new functions.

Coaches register their athletes. Club managers check applications and pay. Managers of city and regional sports committees and communities submit, pay for, check and edit applications. A country administrator manages all applications from the country. An admin manages the database and the functions behind the scenes.

Roles depend on the size of a city. If a city has no committee, the next manager up takes its tasks. A region can have one committee for several cities.

The client gave a brief and a product map built by their PM. The interface had to fit a large amount of data densely, without decoration, in two languages. Success meant a launch and use by the sports federation.

Collapsible status groups and formations keep the country administrator's main screen readable

The country administrator sees all applicants from the whole country: pairs and formations, grouped by application status. In one continuous list, even with headings, an administrator gets lost and cannot tell where anything is.

I made the status groups collapsible. Each formation also opens to show its members only when needed. This saves space and takes load off the administrator.

I explained the decision to the design lead. The PM then accepted it and approved it right away.

Six months of design started from the rules of the competitions

The design lead and I started with references and the old interface. We studied the rules of the competitions, payment conditions from other countries and who may edit what. An interview with the PM and the product map gave us the context. The PM built the map without the designers.

Then the design lead and I made the customer journey map, and I took the layouts into work. We discussed simplifying the interfaces many times, so the screens are easy to take in.

Design and functionality took about half a year. Development then took about another half a year.

The acrobatic calculator fits a whole program on one screen

Program announcement is a required step before a competition starts. The acrobatic calculator is the feature inside it. An organizer or manager sets the conditions for a competition: which tricks and figures a dance has and how many points each element gets. The data is entered and chosen in the calculator and recorded by the system, so the scoring is consistent and fair for every athlete.

It is a complex, mathematical feature, and the specification was strict. My job was to make it clear and easy to navigate. I fitted the whole program into the height of one screen, so the manager does not have to scroll up and down to check it.

The interface is dense and plain on purpose

The client needed a large amount of data on screen, compact and utilitarian. The product uses the ready-made Prime One library, which I adapted and updated.

A person with two roles can switch between them

One person can be a country administrator and a city administrator at the same time. It is rare, but it happens. The interface lets such a person switch roles.

The role menu and the window for choosing a role.

What I took from this project

Sometimes I have to give up air and lightness, when it matters more to the client to fit a large amount of data on screen.

What I would do differently

With more time for research and references, I would choose a design library that fits the product better, or build one from scratch.
