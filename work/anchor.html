01 — Setting

Most business banking is just consumer banking, but louder.

The founders had spent a year talking to SMB owners — bakeries, design studios, dental practices. The pattern was the same. Operators didn't need more financial features. They needed their bank to know they had a payroll on Friday and rent on the first, and to behave accordingly.

Anchor's bet was that a bank for small businesses should feel less like a dashboard and more like a calm assistant who already knows the shape of your month.

02 — Foundations

A balance, a calendar, a verb.

We landed on three primitives the entire product is built from — your operating balance, a calendar of what's coming, and a single verb at the bottom of every screen. Review, send, set aside, pay. Never a row of icons.

We tested twenty variations of this nav model. The winning one had no nav at all — every screen is its own context, and the verb in front of you is the only place to go.

03 — Motion as feedback

Money moves at the speed of real money.

Sending a transfer takes 1.4 seconds in the app — long enough to feel like the bank is doing something, short enough to feel fast. The whole motion language treats transactions as physical objects with weight, not as buttons that flash.

04 — Brand

Calm without being quiet.

The visual system is built on a warm cream and one deep forest green that does all the heavy lifting — confirmations, inflows, status indicators. Numbers are set in italic to feel handwritten, like an entry in a ledger. Sans-serif everywhere else for clarity at twenty-four-point.

05 — Outcome

Fourteen hundred funded accounts in month one.

The beta opened in March 2024 with a waitlist. We funded the first fourteen hundred accounts in the first month and the company closed its Series A on the back of those numbers. Anchor is still the work I'm proudest of — a product that shipped exactly as we drew it.
