FIT FLEET: Fewer Steps, Faster Bookings.

A redesign of the appointment flow for FIT FLEET's management platform — simplifying every step until clients said it just made sense.

The problem

Car park owners were managing fleets through a process that made every service appointment feel harder than it needed to be. The booking flow — used daily to schedule vehicles for authorized FIT SERVICE stations — was slow, unclear, and easy to get wrong.

Users had to move through too many steps without a clear sense of progress. The interface front-loaded information, offered little feedback, and gave no confidence that the booking had been set up correctly.

Approach

We started by walking through the existing flow end to end — every screen, every decision point, every place a user could get stuck or confused. The goal wasn't to redesign everything. It was to find where the friction actually lived.

Working in Miro, we mapped the full journey and identified the steps that caused the most hesitation. From there, I restructured the flow around three principles: show only what the user needs right now, make progress visible at every step, and reduce the number of decisions required to complete a booking. The redesign was built and tested as an interactive prototype in Figma before any development began.

The flow

The new booking experience breaks the process into focused, sequential steps — one clear task per screen. Instead of presenting all fields upfront, the flow guides users through vehicle selection, station choice, date and time, and confirmation as distinct moments. A persistent progress indicator keeps users oriented throughout. Each step is reversible, and the final confirmation screen summarizes everything before submission — so users can catch errors without starting over.

Design decicion

The biggest change wasn't visual — it was structural. The original flow asked users to make too many decisions at once. By separating each stage, we reduced cognitive load without removing any functionality. Visually, the redesign followed a clean, minimal direction: clear typographic hierarchy, generous spacing, and consistent input patterns. Nothing that distracted from the task. The result was an interface that felt like it was on the user's side.

Outcome

Client feedback during interactive prototype testing was immediate and positive. Users found the new flow noticeably easier to navigate and responded strongly to the cleaner interface — both in usability and visual clarity. The redesign was completed and delivered within a 4-week timeline, covering research, flow restructuring, visual design, and prototype testing.
