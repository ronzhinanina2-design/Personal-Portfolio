The thesis

Most education apps treat the learner like a metric to be maximized. More daily streaks, longer sessions, more notifications. Kindling started from the opposite end — what would a design school look like if it was designed for people who already had a job?

The answer turned out to be small. Ten minutes a day. Nine questions. One commentary. Done.

"It's the only learning app I haven't deleted in a year."
— App Store review, four stars.

The lesson

Every lesson follows the same shape — a single principle, three examples in the wild, nine multiple-choice questions, and then a short essay from the curator about why the right answer is right. The whole thing fits in a single bus ride.

What we said no to

Building a consumer app without dark patterns turns out to be a continuous exercise in saying no. No streaks. No push notifications asking you to come back. No "you've earned a gem" animations. No leaderboard. No social. No referral bonus.

Every "no" cost us measurable retention in the prototype phase. We kept saying no. The app shipped at a quarter the size of anything comparable, and held onto its users for almost three times as long.

Outcome

Kindling shipped in August 2022, got featured by Apple a week later, and ran for about two years before my co-founder and I decided to wind it down. It's the project I'd most happily do again, in the next version of my life.
