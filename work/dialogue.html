The problem

The product had grown by adding panels. Every new feature got its own pop-out, its own modal, its own keyboard shortcut. Frontline teammates were spending the first ninety seconds of every shift just laying out their workspace.

Internal NPS for the inbox had fallen four years in a row. Account reviews kept surfacing the same word — chaotic. Leadership wanted "a quieter inbox" and didn't know how to ask for it.

"I love what your product does, I just don't love using it."
— Customer support lead, 2,000-seat customer.

Approach

We started by counting things. Every modal, every popover, every place a teammate could be interrupted. Fifty-three surfaces in the first audit. We cut to twelve, and then argued for a month about which twelve.

The redesign settled on three commitments — one calm canvas, progressive disclosure for everything else, and a keyboard model that worked the same in every view. We held those three principles up against every screen for the next six months.

One canvas

The new inbox is a single resizable canvas with three regions — list, conversation, context. Everything that used to pop out now lives in the right rail, where it can be dismissed but always comes back the way you left it.

Keyboard model

We rebuilt every shortcut around a single principle — a teammate should be able to triage an entire shift without ever touching the mouse. We documented forty-two shortcuts and then cut to eleven. Power users complained for a week, then stopped.

Outcome

Ten weeks after rollout, internal NPS for the inbox crossed forty for the first time in the product's history. Time-to-first-reply on priority conversations fell almost in half. Two of the company's largest customers renewed citing the new inbox.
