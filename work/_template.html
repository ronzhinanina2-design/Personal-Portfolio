02Context

A Series C SaaS company building support tooling for two-sided marketplaces.

Dialogue is the conversation surface — the place where ten thousand customer-facing teammates triage, reply, and hand off threads every day. I joined as lead designer alongside one PM and four engineers. Six months from kickoff to shipped, with a soft rollout over four weeks.

03Problem

The product had grown by adding panels.

Every new feature got its own pop-out, its own modal, its own keyboard shortcut. Teammates spent the first ninety seconds of a shift just laying out their workspace. Internal NPS had fallen four years in a row.

Account reviews kept surfacing the same word — chaotic. Leadership wanted "a quieter inbox" and didn't know how to ask for it.

I love what your product does — I just don't love using it. Support lead · 2,000-seat customer
04Process

Process documentation is intentionally light here. Three decisions shaped the outcome — everything else followed from them.

01

Count everything before redesigning anything.

We audited every modal, popover, and surface in the existing app. Fifty-three. The redesign became an exercise in subtraction, not invention.

02

One canvas, three regions — nothing pops out.

List, conversation, context. Anything that used to open in a modal now lives in the right rail, dismissible but always there when you come back.

03

One keyboard model across every view.

Forty-two shortcuts cut to eleven. Power users complained for a week, then stopped using their mice.

05Solution

The new inbox is one resizable canvas.

Three regions, three jobs. The list shows what's next. The conversation shows what's happening. The context rail shows why it matters. Nothing pops out, nothing competes for the canvas.

The context rail collapses with a single keystroke and remembers its state per teammate, not per conversation. Within two weeks of rollout, 78% of teammates were running with it open by default.

Context rail · expanded
Design note

We surfaced sentiment and CSM in the rail because frontline teammates didn't have time to open a second tab. Putting the relationship in arm's reach changed how replies were written — softer, more specific.

The real problem wasn't the interface — it was that teammates couldn't tell what was theirs to act on.

Triage state moved out of the conversation header and into the list itself. The job a teammate is being asked to do is now legible from forty rows away.

Why a stepper

We replaced the assign-and-route modal with an inline stepper because teammates were dropping off after scrolling. Breaking the decision into three small steps increased completion by 28% in beta.

06Outcome

Ten weeks after rollout, internal NPS crossed forty for the first time.

Time-to-first-reply on priority conversations fell almost in half. Two of the company's largest customers renewed citing the new inbox in their decision notes.
