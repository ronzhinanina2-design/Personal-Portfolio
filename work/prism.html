The problem

The company had three products that looked like they came from three different companies. Marketing, sales, and analytics each had their own button. Their own dropdown. Their own "dark mode" that was a different shade of grey.

The previous design system attempt had ended badly. A monolithic Figma library that nobody opened. Tokens that didn't map to any engineering reality. The word "system" had started to feel like a threat inside the design team.

"Don't build us another library we have to argue with on Monday."
— Engineering lead, kickoff meeting.

The shape

Prism is organized in three layers. Primitives — raw values, the only place a hex code is allowed. Tokens — semantic names that map to primitives. Components — the only thing product designers actually use. Engineers consume tokens, designers consume components, and the mapping in between is the system's only real job.

Governance

The system has one rule and one path. The rule — anything that looks like a component must be a component. The path — anyone can propose a new one, with an RFC and a use case, and every other Friday we triage them.

We made the contribution easy enough that engineers started opening PRs without being asked. Six months in, half of the net-new components came from outside the design team.

Outcome

Two years after launch, Prism still ships every other Friday. It has survived a rebrand and two acquisitions. The original design team is mostly gone. The system is still here, which is the only test that matters for a design system.
